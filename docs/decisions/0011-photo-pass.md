# 0011 Photo pass: photos as attachments, image references, homography

Status: accepted (M11, 2026-11-24). Builds on ADR 0007 and ADR 0010.

## Decision

- **Photos are attachments, not floor data.** A photo is an `attachments` row with `kind: 'photo'`, `floor_id` and `region` = core `AttachmentRegion` (`{ roomId, wallId? }`): the room it was taken in and the wall it faces. The floor document does not change. Devices made from a photo list it in `attachmentIds`, as evidence. Photos are re-encoded in the browser (long side at most 2048 px, JPEG), which also drops EXIF and its GPS position.
- **The model reads the image; code computes metres (principle 7).** The photo pass gets the room's walls (named A, B, … clockwise), the openings on them (`O1`, …) and the devices already there (`E1`, …), and returns image points only, normalised to 0–1000:
  - **references**: corners of walls (where they meet floor and ceiling) and of opening frames, as seen in the photo;
  - **features**: device type (`<domain>.<type>`), wall letter or `ceiling`, box, visible props, count, confidence, and the existing device it is, if any.
- **Placement through a homography.** References with known wall-plane coordinates (metres along the wall from `a`, metres above the floor) fix a homography from the image to the wall plane, fitted by least squares (direct linear transform) over every usable point, 4 at least. A feature's box centre maps to `offset` and `height`. With fewer references, `offset` is interpolated along the wall and `height` is the type's default, and the suggestion is flagged `photo.position-rough`. Ceiling lights are not located from a photo of a wall: they go to the room centroid, flagged the same way. Openings and ceiling height are only corrected when the fit has at least 5 points.
  - **Wall-plane conventions** (`photo/plane.ts`). `u` is metres along the wall's centre line from `a` (as `WallPlacement.offset` and `Opening.offset`), `v` metres above the floor. The viewer stands in the room facing the wall's `side` face, so from the `right` side `a` is the image-left end and from the `left` side `b` is. A wall's corners are the ends of its face in the room (the room polygon's extent along the wall, else the whole wall) at the floor (`v` 0) and the ceiling (the room's ceiling height); an opening's corners are `offset` and `offset + width` mapped the same way, at the sill (0 for doors and passages) and sill + height. A window without a sill has no known height and only helps the rough fallback.
  - **What fits.** Ceiling corners are left out of the fit (their height is what is being checked) and only measure the ceiling. Each wall gets its own fit from its own references; a feature on a wall without 4 usable points (or with all but one on a line, such as only floor-level points) is placed roughly. So is every feature on a wall whose fit has 5 or more distinct points and still misses them by more than 0.1 m (RMS), and a feature whose fitted position lies more than 0.2 m outside the wall face, below the floor or above the ceiling (it is clamped, and flagged rather than silently moved).
  - **Corrections.** They must stand out from the noise of the model's points (Haiku's corners are off by about 1 % of the image). An opening is measured only with a fit from the wall's other references that has at least 5 distinct plane points (points within 3 cm count once), so its own corners do not pull the fit towards the model's size. Its noise `σ` is the fit's RMS residual corrected for the 8 parameters the fit spends (×√(2n/(2n−8))). A width or height is corrected when its mean differs by more than 10 cm and by more than 3 × √(2σ²/k + s²), where k is the number of observed edge pairs and s the jackknife spread of the measurement over fits that each leave one point out (this covers a fit extrapolated from points far from the opening), and when every observed pair on its own (top and bottom edge for width, left and right for height) differs by more than 10 cm in the same direction. The most contradicted opening is taken first and the others are measured again without its corners. A wall where an opening seen in the photo cannot be checked any more once the contradicted ones are left out is ambiguous (with only one other opening and the floor corners, either could be the wrong one) and gets no opening corrections and no ceiling check. The ceiling needs at least two ceiling corners, measured through such fits without the contradicted openings, that agree within 5 cm, and a difference of more than 5 cm and 3σ. Corrections already pending (`pendingCorrections`: opening ids and room ids) are not suggested again. On the synthetic camera at ±1 % noise this gives no false correction in 50 seeds and finds a 20 cm window error in 48 of 50 and a 20 cm ceiling error in 35 of 50 when the wall shows three openings.
  - **De-duplication.** A wall feature is dropped when the model matched it to an existing device, or when a device or pending suggestion of the same type is on the same wall face within 0.3 m along it. Within one photo the 0.3 m rule does not apply, since two real outlets may sit closer: a feature is dropped there only as the same device reported twice (same type, and box IoU above 0.5 or one box's centre inside the other). Ceiling lights are counted instead: of the lights a photo shows of a type, those matched to existing devices are skipped, and the rest less the other lights of that type already in the room or pending go to the slots on the room's long axis not taken by all of those.
- **`ml-matrix` for the fit.** The least-squares solution needs an SVD; `ml-matrix` is pure ESM JavaScript that runs in the browser, Node and Deno (`npm:` in `supabase/functions/deno.json`). A hand-written solve would only cover exactly four points.
- **Detectable types come from the plugins.** `DomainPlugin.aiHints.photo` describes what each type looks like; core `photoDetectables(registry)` turns that into plain data (with the type's placement, default height and ports) that the app sends as part of the pass input. `ai-passes` may not import plugins, and a new domain (the network plugin's data outlets) is detected without changing the pass.

## Why

A vision model is good at naming what it sees and pointing at it, and poor at metric estimates; a homography from known points on the wall is exact for a flat wall and fails visibly (few references, a large residual) instead of quietly. Keeping photos out of the floor document avoids a schema change and keeps big binary references out of the patch stream; the `attachments` table already had `kind` and `region` for this since M2.
