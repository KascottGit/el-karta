# 3D view

The 3D view of the floor editor (ADR 0008). Walls and openings can be moved and resized here with gizmos (ADR 0009); everything else is edited in 2D.

## Geometry (`geometry/`)

Pure functions that turn floor data into boxes, outlines and device transforms. They import no three.js and are unit-tested like core geometry.

**Coordinates.** Everything is in plan coordinates: metres, x right, y down, z up. Each floor is built in floor-local coordinates, with z = 0 at the floor's surface (its `elevation`); `floorBases` gives the z each visible floor is drawn at (its elevation, plus the gaps of the exploded view), and the renderer raises the floor's meshes by it. Changing the visible floors or toggling the exploded view therefore never rebuilds a floor's geometry. The renderer maps a plan point `(x, y, z)` to three.js `(x, z, y)`; that swap mirrors the scene, so it also flips the winding of triangles it builds and the sense of rotations about z.

**Boxes** (`Box`): `center`, `size` and `rotation`. `size.x` runs along the box's own x axis (a wall's length), `size.y` across it (a wall's thickness) and `size.z` is the height. `rotation` is in radians and turns the box's x axis from plan +x towards plan +y, i.e. clockwise on screen in the 2D plan.

**Walls** (`wallPieces`): each wall is split along its length around its openings, so the pieces are a full-height piece between openings, a sill piece under each window and a lintel above each opening, up to `wall.height ?? floor.height`. An opening's `offset` is its start, measured from wall end `a`, and it spans `offset … offset + width` (ADR 0006); openings are clamped to the wall (`openingHole` in `wall-frame.ts`), and overlapping openings cut their union. A wall end that meets another wall's end (within `JOINT_TOLERANCE`, the same test as the 2D wall outline) is extended by half the wall's thickness, which closes L corners and the stem of T corners built from three wall ends. An end is not extended when another wall there continues it in a straight line (a split wall, the crossbar of a T): the two are already continuous, and an extension would overlap the neighbour with coplanar faces and leave a sliver in an opening at the joint. A wall end on the middle of another wall needs no extension: it ends on that wall's centreline, inside its body. Every piece carries its `wallId`.

**Openings** (`openingPieces`): one box per opening that fills its cut-out, clamped by the same `openingHole` as the wall's pieces so piece and hole always match, centred on the wall's centre line: a door leaf (4 cm thick), a window pane (2 cm) between its sill and top, and for a passage a volume as thick as the wall. Each is at most as thick as its wall. An opening on a missing wall or clamped to nothing has no piece. Doors are drawn closed, so the hinge and swing are not shown in 3D.

**Rooms** (`roomSlabs`): the room polygon at z = 0, for the renderer to triangulate. Rooms with an invalid polygon are left out. Ceilings are not drawn.

**Devices** (`deviceTransforms`): a device with a floor placement and a positive `size` is a box standing on the floor at `at`, turned by `placement.rotation` (degrees, clockwise on screen, the same sense as the background's rotation). Every other device is a marker at its `resolvePlacement` position with the shape and colour from `features/devices/appearance.ts` and a `facing` unit vector (out of the wall face, down from a ceiling, up from the floor). A device that cannot be placed is a `flagged` marker at its approximate position, or is skipped when there is none. Devices in a container have no transform.

**Bounds**: `buildFloorGeometry` returns a floor's local `bounds`; `stackedBounds` combines the visible floors at their bases for zoom to fit.

## Rendering

`view-3d.tsx` is the entry point; `EditorWorkspace` loads it with `React.lazy`, so three.js is a separate chunk. It renders a react-three-fiber canvas with `frameloop="demand"` and the device pixel ratio capped at 2, with the floor menu and zoom to fit over it (`view3d-controls.tsx`).

- **Meshes** (`meshes/`): `floorMeshes(floor)` turns a floor's geometry into one merged wall geometry, one merged opening geometry (collection `openings`, with one draw group per kind in `OPENING_KINDS` order, so one mesh draws it with a material per kind) and one merged slab geometry (slabs triangulated with three's `ShapeUtils`), each with a triangle → item table and each item's triangles as one contiguous range, plus one instance group per device shape. It is cached per `FloorData` object, so only a floor whose data changed is rebuilt; `buildCounts()` counts the builds per floor. Devices take the colour of their worst issue, as in 2D. `floorObjects` wraps the meshes in three.js objects that carry a `PickTarget` in `userData.pick`.
- **Selection**: `highlightGeometry(meshes, selection)` copies the selected items' triangles (or a device's template) out of the floor's meshes into a separate mesh, pushed `HIGHLIGHT_INFLATE` out along their normals so it never z-fights. Selecting never touches the merged geometry. Only the editor's floor has a selection. A selected opening highlights its piece; a passage's shows its volume.
- **Materials** (`materials.ts`): shared by every floor. The passage material has `visible: false`: three.js's renderer skips an invisible material, but its `Raycaster` checks neither `Object3D.visible` nor `Material.visible` (only layers), so a tap in a passage's gap still picks the passage. Window glass is transparent and does not write depth.
- **Floors** (`floor-visibility.ts`): the view's own store holds which floors are shown (the editor's floor, all floors of its building, or a chosen set) and the exploded toggle. Buildings do not share coordinates, so only the editor's building is drawn.
- **Picking** (`scene-registry.ts`, `select.ts`): pointer events go through the 2D editor's gesture controller (`createGestureController`), so `DRAG_THRESHOLD` and the multi-touch rules are the same as in 2D, and a tap raycasts the drawn floors. `selectPicked` applies the result like the select tool: a tap selects, Shift or Ctrl/Cmd toggles, empty space clears, and an item on another floor calls `openFloor(floorId, [item])` and then the page's `onOpenFloor`, which navigates and keeps the search parameters. Drags (except on a gizmo handle), pinches and the wheel go to drei's `OrbitControls`.
- **Camera**: opening the view and the zoom to fit button frame the visible floors (`fitCamera`). Until the camera is moved or something is selected, a resize fits again, like the 2D editor's `fitted`.
- **Gizmos** (`gizmos/`, ADR 0009): a single selected wall or opening on the editor's floor shows handles, drawn over everything (no depth test) and scaled every frame to a constant size on screen (`HANDLE_PX`). A wall has a double arrow on each face at mid-length and mid-height (`move-left`, `move-right`: move along the normal with `moveWall`'s plan), a sphere at each top end (`corner-a`, `corner-b`: the corner dragged in the horizontal plane through it with `moveEnds`, snapped like 2D with `snapPoint`) and a cube at mid-top (`height`). An opening has a double arrow at its centre (`slide`), cubes at its side edges (`start` at `offset`, `end` at `offset + width`), at its top and, for a window, at its sill, all on its clamped cut-out (`openingHole`). Each handle is constrained to an axis (along the wall, its left normal, or up) or to the horizontal plane through it. The maths is pure (`ray.ts`: the closest point between the pointer ray and an axis, the ray's hit on a level plane, metres per pixel at a depth), and so is each handle's mapping from a drag to the changed items (`drag.ts`, which calls the 2D functions in `walls/` and `openings/resize.ts`). Snapping and rounding follow the 2D toggle and Alt; the snap reach is `SNAP_PX` converted to metres at the handle's depth.
- **Gizmo drags** (`gizmos/controller.ts`): `Interaction` listens in the capture phase, so a first press within `GRAB_PX` of a handle (an arrow along its shaft; an edge handle on a narrow opening's arrow wins) turns the orbit controls off before they see it and captures the pointer; any other press behaves as before. Past the drag threshold each move previews the change: the dragged floor is drawn from a preview `FloorData` in `gizmoStore` (`previewFloor`, only the changed collection copied), which the mesh cache builds like any other data, so only that floor rebuilds (about 7 ms per move on the busiest floor of the large synthetic project; `gizmos/perf.test.ts`). Release applies one edit through the model store, and only a change computed on the floor's current data. While a handle is held the controller follows the model: a change to the floor (undo, a remote patch) recomputes the preview from the last pointer position, and when the item is gone or the change is refused the drag is cancelled. Escape, a second finger, a new selection and another floor cancel it too. Only a first pointer can grab a handle, so a finger joining an orbit or pinch never stops it. A tap on a handle does nothing.
- **Dev hook** (`dev-hook.ts`): in dev builds the view sets `window.__view3d` with `screenPosition(item)` (a point where a tap picks the item, or null when it is out of view or covered), `builds()`, `highlighted()` and `gizmoHandle(name)` (a handle's centre and the unit screen direction that drags it the positive way). The e2e helpers in `apps/web/e2e/view3d.ts` use it.
